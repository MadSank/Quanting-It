Type a sentence. Watch it come apart.

Every letter becomes bits, then light, then noise only Bob can undo.

Your sentence never leaves this page. The hashing and encryption run in your browser.

your words

Plain letters. In the first version of Quatinit, this is exactly what crossed the wire.

a fingerprint

The sentence’s SHA-256 hash, cut to 32 bits. Each bit picks which one-time key Alice reveals to sign it.

light

Photons in two bases build a shared key. Bob keeps the ones where his basis matched Alice’s.

someone looks

Eve measures in the middle. A quarter of what she touches arrives wrong, and Bob notices.

noise

With a clean key, AES-256-GCM turns your words into this. It is all an eavesdropper keeps.

your words, again

Bob checks the signature, opens the seal and reads it.

light

BB84 builds the key from single photons, 40,000 pulses a session. Above 11% errors the key is discarded.

qsc/bb84.py

lattices

ML-KEM-768 and ML-DSA-65, the NIST post-quantum standards, cover what photons cannot. Every handshake message is signed.

qsc/channel.py

one-time keys

The signature scheme of Gottesman and Chuang: 32 positions, 128-bit keys, each checked by a destructive SWAP test.

qds_signer.py, swap_test.py
